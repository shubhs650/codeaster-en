# Operators AFFE_CHAR_MECA AFFE_CHAR_MECA_C AFFE_CHAR_MECA_F

## 1 Purpose

Assign loads and boundary conditions on a mechanical model.

- For AFFE_CHAR_MECA, the values assigned depend on no parameter and are defined by real values.
- For AFFE_CHAR_MECA_C, the values assigned depend on no parameter and are defined by complex values.
- For AFFE_CHAR_MECA_F, the values assigned are functions of one or more parameters in the set {INST, X, Y, Z,XF,YF,ZF}.

## 3 General

### 3.1 Principles

There are three main categories of operands:

- The operands that apply kinematic conditions or Dirichlet loads, that is, relations between the degrees of freedom. In AFFE_CHAR_MECA, these conditions are applied by Lagrange-multiplier enforcement (double Lagrange method, see [R2.03.01]);
- The operands that apply «force» loads or Neumann loads, applied in weak form, which implies a numerical integration scheme. Some loads require boundary elements in the model;
- The operands that apply special loads, including mixed Dirichlet/Neumann ones.

Most operands are built on the same principle:

- The location where the boundary conditions apply is given by the standard keywords GROUP_NO, GROUP_MA and sometimes SANS_GROUP_NO and SANS_GROUP_MA.
- The components assigned are given; they fall into three groups:
- Standard components of the physical quantity considered. This is the quantity DEPL_R (or DEPL_C or DEPL_F), representing the degrees of freedom of the mechanical problem (see § 5.1);
- Combined components DNOR and DTAN, which build a combination of the components of the quantity DEPL_R based on the tangents and the normal;
- Force, moment or pressure components, using either the quantity FORC_R (or FORC_C or FORC_F) or the quantity PRES_R (or PRES_C or PRES_F);
- The components assigned must be of the right type for the operator used:
- Real type for the operator AFFE_CHAR_MECA;
- Complex type for the operator AFFE_CHAR_MECA_C;
- Function type (created in particular by one of the operators DEFI_FONCTION, DEFI_NAPPE or DEFI_CONSTANTE) for the operator AFFE_CHAR_MECA_F. This holds with one exception: the argument of COEF_MULT for the factor keyword LIAISON_DDL in AFFE_CHAR_MECA_F must be of real type.

For the loads LIAISON_DDL, LIAISON_MAIL, LIAISON_GROUP and LIAISON_UNIF, the list of applicable components is restricted to the following degrees of freedom {liste_dof}.: 'DX', 'DY', 'DZ', 'DRX', 'DRY', 'DRZ', 'PRES', 'PHI', 'GONF', 'TEMP', 'PRE1', 'PRE2', 'GRX', 'DRGX', 'DRGY', 'DRGZ', 'PSI', 'UI2', 'UI3', 'VI2', 'VI3', 'WI2', 'WI3', 'UO2', 'UO3', 'VO2', 'VO3', 'WO2', 'WO3', 'UI4', 'UI5', 'VI4', 'VI5', 'WI4', 'WI5', 'UO4', 'UO5', 'VO4', 'VO5', 'WO4', 'WO5', 'UI6', 'UO6', 'VI6', 'VO6', 'WI6', 'WO6', 'WO', 'WI1', 'WO1', 'H1X', 'H1Y', 'H1Z', 'H1PRE1', 'K1', 'K2', 'K3', 'V11', 'V12', 'V13', 'V21', 'V22', 'V23', 'V31', 'V32', 'V33', 'PRES11', 'PRES12', 'PRES13', 'PRES21', 'PRES22', 'PRES23', 'PRES31', 'PRES32', 'PRES33', 'LH1', 'GLIS', 'HHO_U1', 'HHO_U2', 'HHO_U3', 'HHO_U4', 'HHO_U5', 'HHO_U6', 'HHO_V1', 'HHO_V2', 'HHO_V3', 'HHO_V4', 'HHO_V5', 'HHO_V6', 'HHO_W1', 'HHO_W2', 'HHO_W3', 'HHO_W4', 'HHO_W5' and 'HHO_W6'.

### 3.2 Assumptions and limitations

Besides the assumptions and limitations specific to each load, there are general assumptions, recalled here.

#### 3.2.1 Linearity of the kinematic relations

A kinematic relation writes an equation of the form: r ∑ U = (1) i i i=1 With U the list of the r degrees of freedom,  the coefficients and  the right-hand side. i i The kinematic relations of code_aster are linear relations, that is: <!-- equation -->

<!-- page 8 -->

- They cannot depend a priori on the deformation or motion of the structure: they only hold under the small-perturbation assumption, unless stated otherwise (see LIAISON_SOLIDE );
- The coefficients  of the linear relation cannot be functions of time, because the matrix i B of the Dirichlet conditions is constant during the whole transient. These coefficients can, however, be functions of the initial geometry;
- The right-hand side  can be a function of time or of the initial geometry;

#### 3.2.2 Neumann loads

Some Neumann loads can be nonlinear and, in particular, depend on the deformation of the structure. Such loads are commonly called follower loads. In this case, however, the problem becomes nonlinear, so a suitable calculation operator such as STAT_NON_LINE or DYNA_NON_LINE must be used, and these loads must be declared as followers (see [U4.51.03]).

Most Neumann loads (except FORCE_NODALE) are applied in weak form, that is, with a numerical quadrature formula. Also, a Neumann load and a Dirichlet load cannot be applied at the same time on the same node in the same direction. As a result, there can be a difference between the theoretical solution and the finite element solution.

For example, on a structure with too coarse a mesh, the sum of the nodal forces corresponding to the gravity load can differ from the actual weight, the difference corresponding roughly to the number of clamped nodes of the structure.

Refining the mesh minimizes this difference. The finite elements on which kinematic conditions are imposed can also be made small enough for their weight to be negligible compared with that of the whole structure. Another solution is to duplicate the nodes on which the kinematic condition is imposed and, for example, use a LIAISON_DDL between the two nodes, or use discrete elements.

### 3.5 Choice of units

For Neumann loads, the forces must be given per mesh unit for line forces, per mesh unit squared for surface forces and per mesh unit cubed for body forces), consistently with the definition of the material properties (Young's modulus, for example). In the axisymmetric case, the forces given are referred to a sector of 1 radian (divide the actual load by 2π ). <!-- equation -->

### 3.6 Large transformations

#### 3.6.1 The issue

<!-- page 11 -->

When a Dirichlet load is applied on a structure in large transformations (large displacements, large rotations), make sure that this load is applicable when the small-perturbation assumption no longer holds.

This is naturally the case for the following loads:

- DDL_IMPO; but with AFFE_CHAR_MECA_F , use functions that represent the actual imposed displacement (generally curvilinear). Otherwise, the time interpolation goes through «wrong» intermediate states;

```text
•  LIAISON_MAIL + TYPE_RACCORD=’MASSIF’ ;
•  LIAISON_UNIF ;
•  LIAISON_OBLIQUE ;
•  LIAISON_DDL ;
•  LIAISON_CHAM_NO ;
•  CHAMNO_IMPO ;
```

- LIAISON_SOLIDE, provided the follower-load assumption is applied (see § 3.6 ).

For the following loads, however, the large-transformation assumption does not apply and gives wrong results:

- FACE_IMPO with DNOR=f(t): the «normal» changes under large rotations;
- LIAISON_MAIL + TYPE_RACCORD=’COQUE_MASSIF’ and LIAISON_MAIL + 'MASSIF_COQUE' ;
- LIAISON_ELEM ;

```text
•  LIAISON_RBE3.
```

#### 3.6.2 Follower loads

For the nonlinear operators (STAT_NON_LINE and DYNA_NON_LINE), some loads can be «followers», that is, their application depends on the displacement and therefore changes at each Newton iteration. The user must then say so with the operand TYPE_CHARGE in the factor keyword EXCIT of these commands (see [U4.51.03]). Declaring the load as a follower sometimes adds a contribution to the stiffness matrix (see for example [R3.03.04]) and can make it nonsymmetric. For the load EVOL_CHAR (see §7.7), the loads need not be declared as followers: they are followers by default. Declaring it only enables the additional matrix contribution and so acts on the convergence rate (not on the accuracy of the result).

### 3.7 Naming the topological entities to which loads are assigned

In general, when the entities on which values must be assigned are defined:

- On a single node: use the operand GROUP_NO, which must of course hold only one node;
- On a list of nodes: use the operand GROUP_NO, or the operand GROUP_MA, or TOUT=’OUI’ to assign on the whole mesh;
- On a single element: use the operand GROUP_MA, which must of course hold only one element;
- On a list of elements: use the operand GROUP_MA, or TOUT=’OUI’ to assign on the whole mesh;

Some keywords need several topological entities (node groups facing each other, for example); in that case the names can vary slightly (GROUP_NO_2, GROUP_MA_ESCL, etc.). In most keywords, nodes or elements can be excluded with operators of type SANS_*. This avoids redefining groups in the mesh or in the command DEFI_GROUP.

### 3.8 Overloading and persistence rules

To define the assignment domain as simply as possible, the overloading rule defined in the document [U1.03.00] is used; its principles are:

- The assignments superpose the effects of the different loads;
<!-- page 12 -->

- In case of conflict, the last load wins over the previous ones;

If, for example, the user writes:

```text
FORCE_FACE=_F(GROUP_MA=’G1’,FX=12.),
PRES_REP=_F(GROUP_MA=’G1’,PRES=13.)
```

And if the normal for G1 points along X, then everything happens as if one had written:

```text
FORCE_FACE=_F(GROUP_MA=’G1’, FX=25.)
```

The overloading rule above must be completed by another rule that says what happens when several quantities can be assigned for each occurrence of a load. Take for example:

```text
FORCE_INTERNE=(
   _F(TOUT     = ’OUI’,  FX = 1.              ),
   _F(GROUP_MA = ’GM1’,            F Y = 2.   ),
)
```

The overloading rule says that the second occurrence of FORCE_INTERNE overloads the first. But what is FX on an element of GM1? Was it erased by the second occurrence? If only the overloading rule is applied, FX is not defined on GM1. A second rule, called the persistence rule, therefore says that when the overloading rule is applied on occurrences, the components that are not overloaded are kept. Applying the persistence rule to the example, FX keeps the value assigned before. All the elements of the model therefore have a value for FX, and the elements of GM1 have a value for both FX and FY

### 3.9 Definition of the coordinate systems

Most loads are defined in the global coordinate system of the mesh, except:

- For structural elements (§3.9.2);
- For the keywords DNOR, DTAN and the pressure loads. In this case, the normals and tangents must be defined (see §3.9.1), possibly using the command MODI_MAILLAGE ;
- When the keyword ANGL_NAUT can be used (see §3.9.3); In the other cases, functions of space can generally be defined (as long as the assumptions of §3.2 hold).

#### 3.9.1 Normals and tangents to the elements

The standard definition of the normals and tangents for each type of boundary element is:

- For segment elements in 2D, the tangent is the one defined by the segment oriented by its first two nodes; the normal n is then such that n,t form a right-handed coordinate system 2 t 1 2 1 t n
- For triangle or quadrangle elements in 3D, the orientation of the normal n is the one corresponding to the direct sense of the element description.

n n 3 4 3

1 2 1 2 If DNOR (or DTAN) is given, the normal (or tangent) at a node is the mean of the normals or tangents of the elements on which the boundary conditions are assigned and that share this node (except for curved quadratic elements, where the normal is computed exactly at every point). n n n

The operator MODI_MAILLAGE ensures a continuous orientation of the normal on the boundaries of solid continuous-medium elements.

#### 3.9.2 Structural elements

Structural elements (beams, plates and shells) have their own local coordinate system, defined in the documentation of the command AFFE_CARA_ELEM [U4.42.01].

```text

```

This keyword says whether the user wants to duplicate the Lagrange multipliers used to enforce the boundary conditions in the assembled matrix. In practice, duplicating the Lagrange multipliers allows linear solvers that do not pivot to be used. Not duplicating them reduces the number of degrees of freedom of the problem (and so the size of the problem to solve), but this is limited to the solvers MUMPS and Petsc.

Note that the two Lagrange multipliers associated with the relation are placed so that the first comes before all the terms involved in the relation and the second after, in the assembled matrix. This choice has the advantage of giving an assembled matrix with a smaller profile, but the drawback that a singularity can appear in the matrix. Since the numbering can in any case be changed by the renumbering of the external solver chosen, this detail stays purely technical.

### 5.2 Conflicts between degrees of freedom

Within the same AFFE_CHAR_MECA, including between its occurrences, the command checks, by applying the overloading and persistence rules, that the boundary conditions are not redundant (see §3.8). Such a situation would give a singular matrix in the calculation. However, if the same boundary condition is given twice by two different calls to AFFE_CHAR_MECA (for example, with two imposed displacement values), this gives a singular matrix. Kinematic boundary conditions are always imposed on nodes, not on elements.

### 5.3 Pairing operations

#### 5.3.1 Node-to-node pairing (compatible meshes)

This type of pairing (used for example in LIAISON_GROUP or LIAISON_COQUE) builds pairs of nodes. It works the same way as in AFFE_CHAR_THER. First, the two lists of nodes  and  to put 1 2 face to face (that is, to pair) are built for each occurrence of the factor keyword. Once redundancies are removed, the two lists of nodes obtained must have the same length. The pairs of facing nodes are determined in several steps: <!-- equation -->

- For each node N1 of the first list, the image node N2= f N1 in the second list is searched for. If f is not injective (a node N2 is the image of two distinct nodes N1 and N3), the following error message is issued: There is a conflict in the node pairing. The node N1 faces both the node N2 and the node N3 .
- For each node N2 of the second list, the image node N1=gN2 in the first list is searched for. If g is not injective (a node N1 is the image of two distinct nodes N2 and N3), the following error message is issued: There is a conflict in the node pairing. The node N1 faces both the node N2 and the node N3 .
- The command checks that g= f −1 , that is, that the pairs obtained in steps a) and b) are the same (a bijection f between the two lists of nodes is wanted). If f is not surjective, the following error message is issued: There is a conflict in the node pairing. The node N1 is not the image of any node by the inverse mapping.

For a given node N, the image node f N is the node of the other list that is closest to N. To ease the pairing, especially for particular geometries (where the boundaries  and  could «almost» be deduced from each other 1 2 by a translation combined with a rotation), a virtual geometric transformation of the first node group (translation and rotation) can be applied before the distances are computed (keywords TRAN, CENTRE and ANGL_NAUT). <!-- equation -->

<!-- page 20 -->

In the pairs of facing nodes, the order of the nodes matters. If for the first occurrence of the factor keyword a node N belonged to the first node group and a node M to the second, and for the second occurrence of the same factor keyword it is the reverse, the pairing gives the pairs N ,M and M ,N . They are not removed by the redundancy detection, but the matrix obtained is singular. It is therefore recommended to keep the same logic when describing facing boundaries. <!-- equation -->

#### 5.3.2 Element-to-node pairing (incompatible meshes)

In the rest of this section, we speak of the «secondary» face (FACE2) and the «main» face (FACE1). The two faces are «glued» by writing linear relations between the degrees of freedom of the two faces. The displacements of the nodes of the secondary face are tied to the displacements of their projections on the main face. For each node of the secondary face, 2 (in 2D) or 3 (in 3D) linear relations are written (see the operator PROJ_CHAMP for more details). The principle of the connection is to eliminate the secondary degrees of freedom by writing them as linear relations of the main degrees of freedom. The problem has some symmetry, and one might think that the main and secondary sides can be chosen at random. In fact, two points need attention:

- The syntax is not symmetric: on the secondary side, the user gives the nodes to «weld», whereas on the main side, elements must be given. Also, the main elements are (for now) of one topological dimension [higher than] what would be natural. For example, for a 2D mesh, the surfaces to glue are lines, and one might expect the main elements to be segments. The code expects surface elements (quadrangles and triangles).
- It is better (from a mechanical point of view) to choose the more finely meshed surface as the secondary surface, just as it is better to multiply the weld points when welding two sheets.

Remarks:

  - In 3D, do not give surface main elements, but the volume elements adjacent to the face. The elements given are «candidates» for the search of facing points. Giving too many is not a problem. In the same way, in 2D, the «main» elements must be surface elements (QUAD, TRIA), not line elements.
  - When gluing a mesh of linear P1 elements onto a quadratic P2 mesh, it is generally recommended to choose as «secondary» face the face

that is quadratic .

#### 5.13.1 Use and examples

The keyword LIAISON_MAIL defines linear relations to «glue» two «boundaries» of a structure. In the rest of this section, we speak of the «secondary» face (FACE2) and the «main» face (FACE1). The two faces are «glued» by writing linear relations between the degrees of freedom of the two faces, see §5.3.2.

#### 5.13.2 Some remarks and precautions of use

- If FACE1 and FACE2 do not coincide geometrically but there is an isometry (rotation and translation) between the two, the user must define this isometry (the one that transforms FACE2 into FACE1) with the keywords CENTRE / ANGL_NAUT / TRAN.
- A «classical» use of this feature is, for example, gluing a mesh of linear P1 elements onto a quadratic P2 mesh. In this case it is generally recommended to choose the quadratic face as the «secondary» face.
- The keyword LIAISON_MAIL is in principle meant to tie two surfaces that are a priori disjoint. Sometimes this is not the case, and a secondary node can belong to one of the main elements. The linear relation the problem tries to write then becomes a tautology ( X=X ), which gives a zero pivot in the factorization. To avoid this, the relations tying a secondary node to its main element are not written if:
- This node belongs to the connectivity of the element;
- And the keywords CENTRE / ANGL_NAUT / TRAN were not used.
- Be aware that for each occurrence of LIAISON_MAIL, all the secondary nodes are a priori tied to the main elements even if the projection distances are large (warnings are, however, issued in that case). This behavior can be changed with the keyword DISTANCE_MAX.
- If one writes:

```text
LIAISON_MAIL = ( _F(GROUP_MA_ESCL=’GE’,G ROUP_MA_MAIT= ’GM1’),
                 _F(GROUP_MA_ESCL=’GE’,G ROUP_MA_MAIT= ’GM2’))
```

It would be a mistake to think that the program sorts in GE the nodes close to GM1 and those close to GM2 . In this example, the nodes of GE are eliminated twice, and a zero-pivot problem can be expected in the factorization.

<!-- page 32 -->

The user must write:

```text
LIAISON_MAIL = _F(GROUP_MA_ESCL=’GE’, GROUP_MA_MAIT=(’GM1’,’GM2’))
```

The keyword LIAISON_SOLIDE models a non-deformable part of a structure. Linear relations are imposed between the degrees of freedom of the nodes of this non-deformable part so that the relative displacements between these nodes are zero, and the displacements are possibly imposed to the values resulting from the translation and/or rotation. A solid is determined by the position of one of its points and of a coordinate system at this point. The relations are written from the vector formula expressing a rigid-body motion under small rotations: <!-- equation -->

  -  uM=uAA∧AM (6)

where A is an arbitrary node of the solid. For more details, see document [R3.03.02]. LIAISON_SOLIDE can be used under large transformations in STAT_NON_LINE (since the problem becomes nonlinear) only by requiring the load to be a follower load (TYPE_CHARGE=’SUIV’, see [U4.51.03]).

```text
◊   DIST_MIN
```

This keyword defines a distance (in mesh units) below which the mesh points are considered coincident. This distance is also used to determine whether points are aligned, that is whether they lie inside a cylinder of diameter less than dmin. By default, dmin=0.001×armin, where armin is the smallest edge of the mesh. <!-- equation -->

Remarks: If an element has all its nodes in a zone made rigid by LIAISON_SOLIDE, its strain is zero. The stress state is then also zero, as are the generalized forces for a structural element. If the list of nodes to be tied contains only aligned nodes carrying no rotational degree of freedom, then, if the system is to undergo a rotation, a non-aligned dummy node must be added to the list. The other solution is to work under the assumption of large rotations and large displacements (TYPE_CHARGE='SUIV'): the rigid tie is then explicitly required to keep the distances constant.
