# CALC_MODES operator

## 1 Purpose

Compute the vibration mode shapes or the Euler buckling modes of a mechanical structure.

To do this, the operator CALC_MODES solves a modal problem that can be either generalized (GEP) [R5.01.01] , or quadratic (QEP) [R5.01.02] .

The user specifies the mode search criterion (for example: in a band; near given values; etc.) with the keyword OPTION. Depending on the value of this keyword, the general calculation method (simultaneous iterations or inverse power) is then determined automatically.

Within each of these two general methods, variants exist:

- simultaneous iterations: subspace-type method (Bathe & Wilson, Lanczos or Sorensen), global QR-type method (QZ for small problems) ;
- inverse power: direct method or method accelerated by the Rayleigh quotient. These variants are accessible with a factor keyword SOLVEUR_MODAL.

For a calculation of vibration mode shapes, post-processing can also be done: normalization of the modes according to a given criterion, filtering of the modes according to a given criterion, production of the modal parameters ...

This operator produces a result object mode_meca_* (dynamic case) or mode_flamb (Euler buckling case, GEP only) or mode_gene, depending on the value given to the keyword TYPE_RESU and on the type of the input matrices of the modal problem. The result object mode_meca_* , or mode_flamb , contains the eigenvalues and eigenvectors, and modal parameters, see [R5.01.03] . As a first approach, it is enough to give the following parameters: OPTION to define the mode search criterion, TYPE_RESU, the input matrices of the modal problem MATR_*, CALC_FREQ (or CALC_CHAR_CRIT ).

### 3.1 Principles

This operator solves the following generalized eigenvalue problem (GEP) [R5.01.01] : Find ,x such that Ax=Bx, x≠0, where A and B are real matrices, symmetric or not. To model hysteretic damping in the study of the free vibrations of a structure, the matrix A can be complex symmetric [U2.06.03] [R5.05.04]. In mechanics, this type of problem corresponds in particular to: <!-- equation -->

- The study of the free vibrations of an undamped, non-rotating structure. For this structure, the smallest eigenvalues, or those that lie in a given interval, are sought, to know whether an exciting force can create a resonance. In this case, the matrix A is the material stiffness matrix, written K , real symmetric (possibly augmented by the geometric stiffness matrix written K , if the structure is prestressed), and B is the g mass or inertia matrix written M (real symmetric). The eigenvalues obtained are the squares of the angular frequencies associated with the frequencies sought. The system to solve can be written  KK  x=Mx g

A B where =2 f2 is the square of the angular frequency  , f the natural frequency and x the associated displacement eigenvector. The mode shapes handled ,x are real-valued. This type of problem is activated by the keyword TYPE_RESU='DYNAMIQUE' and generates a data structure of type mode_meca, mode_acou or mode_gene (depending on the type of the input data). <!-- equation -->

- The search for linear buckling modes. Within linearized theory, assuming a priori that the stability phenomena are properly described by the system of equations obtained by assuming that the displacement depends linearly on the level of

load, the search for the buckling mode x associated with this critical load level =­ . This reduces to a generalized eigenvalue problem of the form:  KK  x=0⇔Kx=Kx g g <!-- equation -->

A B with K the material stiffness matrix and K the geometric stiffness matrix. The g modes handled ,x are real-valued. This type of problem is activated by the keyword TYPE_RESU='MODE_FLAMB' and generates a data structure of type mode_flamb. <!-- equation -->

This operator also handles the study of the dynamic stability of a structure in the presence of damping and/or gyroscopic effects. This leads to the solution of a higher-order modal problem, called quadratic (QEP) [R5.01.02]. Complex eigenvalues and eigenvectors (λ,x) are then sought.

This reduces the norm of the final residual. This is what the post-processing keyword 'AMELIORATION' does, see § 3.13.

Thus, even if the options of the simultaneous iteration methods ('PLUS_PETITE/GRANDE', 'CENTRE' and 'BANDE') are often preferable, up to about ten modes can be computed with the options of the power-type methods: 'SEPARE', 'AJUSTE' or 'PROCHE'. To compute tens or even hundreds of modes, the option 'BANDE' should be preferred when possible. This improves the robustness, quality and performance of the calculation. In the standard case of a real symmetric GEP, ideally the calculation should be organized in several sub-bands that each hold between 20 and 60 modes, with, if possible, a split that is even in number of modes (an "imbalance" below 3 1 ).

To do this, one can proceed in several steps:

- Calibrate the zones of interest with an initial call to INFO_MODE [U4.52.01] on a given list of frequencies2 (resp. critical loads),
- Look at the numbers of mode shapes shown in the message file (or in the generated sd_table),
- Run one or more CALC_MODES calculations again with OPTION='BANDE', trying to balance the bands.

Remark:


This keyword defines the nature of the modal problem to handle: search for vibration frequencies (classic dynamic case with or without damping and gyroscopic effects) or search for critical loads (linear buckling theory, GEP only). Depending on this class, the results are displayed and stored differently in the data structure:

- In dynamics (TYPE_RESU='DYNAMIQUE', the frequencies are ordered by increasing modulus of their distance to the shift (see [R5.01.01/02], §3.8/2.5). This is the value of the access variable NUME_ORDRE of the data structure. The other access variable, NUME_MODE, equals the true modal position of the eigenvalue in the spectrum (determined by the Sturm test, see [R5.01.04]). This Sturm test is allowed only in GEP with real modes (real symmetric matrices); in the other cases (GEP with complex modes and QEP), NUME_MODE=NUME_ORDRE is set.
- In buckling (TYPE_RESU='MODE_FLAMB'), the eigenvalues are stored in increasing algebraic order. The variables NUME_ORDRE and NUME_MODE take the same value, equal to this order.
- General case (TYPE_RESU='GENERAL') : same as for the buckling case.

Remark:

  - TYPE_RESU='GENERAL' solves an eigenvalue problem for a general matrix system. For now its scope is limited to standard GEPs (real symmetric matrices). Its only difference from MODE_FLAMB is therefore in the naming of the matrices: MATR_A / MATR_B rather than MATR_RIGI / MATR_RIGI_GEOM..

The calculation is then split into several frequency sub-bands (see operand FREQ). On each of these sub-bands, a modal solver does the associated mode search. These two calculation building blocks (modal solver and linear solver) are the steps that drive the memory and time consumption of the calculation. They are what must be targeted to reduce the calculation costs of this operator significantly.

The organization of the modal calculation on distinct sub-bands offers an ideal framework for parallelism here: distribution of large, almost independent calculations11. This parallelism saves a lot of time, at the price of extra memory12.

If enough processors are available (more than the number of non-empty sub-bands), a second level of parallelism can then be switched on through the linear solver (if METHODE='MUMPS' has been chosen). It will keep saving time but, above all, it will offset the extra memory of the first level, or even reduce the sequential memory peak notably.

However, if the parallel distribution of the data, generated before CALC_MODES (through AFFE_MODELE/MODI_MODELE), was produced with parameters other than the default values (keywords DISTRIBUTION + CHARGE_PROC0_MA or NB_SOUS_DOMAINE/PARTITIONNEUR/CHARGE_PROC0_SD activated) and the exact same distribution is wanted again, an explicit call to MODI_MODELE must be made after CALC_MODES. Otherwise, the parallel partitioning that follows CALC_MODES will use the default values. This is often the best-performing scenario.
  - In mode NIVEAU_PARALLELISME='COMPLET', all the eigenvectors found are communicated at the end of CALC_MODES. So the distinction15 between the values STURM='LOCAL' or ' GLOBAL' no longer makes functional sense. This does not matter, since the default mode to prefer is ' GLOBAL'.

For the practical use of parallelism, see the generic document [U2.08.06] on parallelism, and the dedicated paragraph of [U2.06.01] on modal calculation.

### 3.15 Keyword AMELIORATION

```text
◊    AMELIORATION=           /'NON'            [DEFAUT]
                             /'OUI'
```

Keyword that automatically improves the quality of the computed modes: mainly the mode shapes (which shows as a decrease of the error norm) and the eigenvalues. This improvement is done by chaining two modal calculations: a first one, preferably done by a simultaneous iteration method (keyword OPTION='BANDE' or 'PLUS_PETITE' or 'CENTRE' or 'TOUT'); the second is done transparently for the user, with the inverse iteration method (OPTION='PROCHE'), taking as input the eigenvalues estimated by the first calculation.

Remarks:

  - The quality of the modes is usually already sufficient after a first calculation. This option, despite its cost, can however be very useful for complicated models.
  - When this improvement option is active, the post-check tests, normally done at the end of the first step (according to the settings of VERI_MODE), are done here only at the end of the second.

## 5 Execution phase

- When the simultaneous iteration method is used (i.e. if OPTION='BANDE' or 'CENTRE' or 'PLUS_PETITE' or 'PLUS_GRANDE' or 'TOUT'): if the keyword DIM_SOUS_ESPACE (under the factor keyword SOLVEUR_MODAL) is not given or is set to a value strictly lower than the number of requested modes nf (operand NMAX_FREQ or NMAX_CHAR_CRIT), the operator automatically computes an admissible dimension for the projection subspace with the empirical formulas (see §3.7.1.4):

```text
METHODE='SORENSEN'
ndim=MIN(MAX(2+nf,mse*nf),nactif) avecmse=2 pardéfaut.
METHODE='TRI_DIAG'
ndim=MIN(MAX(7+nf,mse*nf),nactif )avecmse=4 pardéfaut.
METHODE='JACOBI'
ndim=MIN(MAX(7+nf,mse*nf),nactif)avecmse=2 pardéfaut.
```

where nactif is the number of active degrees of freedom (that is, the total number of degrees of freedom minus the number of LAGRANGE degrees of freedom and minus the number of linear relations that link degrees of freedom together, [R5.01.01] §3.2) and mse is the proportionality factor set by COEF_DIM_ESPACE.

If a GEP is solved, the dimension of the subspace is doubled. The values of these various parameters are printed in the MESSAGE file.

- For the option 'SEPARE' : having obtained an interval that brackets an eigenvalue, the middle of the interval is taken for the calculation of the mode. During the calculation of the mode, the value of the eigenvalue is refined further. This is the result of the inverse iteration proper.
- For the option 'AJUSTE ' : if separation is not possible and a given interval contains more than one eigenvalue, the adjustment method is not applied to that interval. On the other hand, during the calculation of the modes, reorthogonalizations with respect to the preceding modes contained in the interval are done (this makes it possible to compute modes associated with a multiple eigenvalue).

## 6 Modal parameters/ Norm of the modes/ Modal position

On output of this operator, the real or complex mode shapes are normalized to the largest component that is not a LAGRANGE multiplier. To choose another norm, use the factor keyword NORM_MODE inside CALC_MODES, or the command NORM_MODE [U4.52.11] after CALC_MODES (which gives access to more features).

For a dynamic calculation, the data structure mode_meca_* , and mode_ gene , contains, in addition to the vibration frequencies and the associated mode shapes, modal parameters (generalized mass, generalized stiffness, participation factor, effective mass). The definition of these parameters is in [R5.01.03].

For a linear buckling calculation, the data structure mode_flamb contains only the critical loads and the associated deformed shapes.

For a generalized dynamic calculation with real symmetric matrices, the modal position corresponds to the position of the mode in the whole spectrum defined by the initial matrices. In all the other cases, the modal positions are assigned from 1 to nf (nf being the number of retained modes) by sorting them in increasing algebraic order. All the modal positions are therefore positive.

## 7 Optimization of CPU performance

### 7.1 Parallelism of the linear solver

The modal calculation uses a linear solver. If the linear solver MUMPS is chosen with the factor keyword SOLVEUR, operand METHODE='MUMPS', it can run in parallel. This requires a parallel version of Code_Aster and the number of processors to use. Empirically, one processor per 105 to 106 degrees of freedom of the model gives good performance. In parallel, the setting RENUM='QAMD' is also recommended.

### 7.2 Computing the modes by sub-bands

If the problem to handle is a GEP and many modes are sought in a given band, or the number of degrees of freedom of the model is large (> 105), it is advised to split the global search band into several sub-bands of 40 to 80 modes, as balanced as possible. The calculation will then be more robust and faster, even when calling one CALC_MODES per sub-band sequentially. For ease of use, the calculations on each sub-band can be done automatically and transparently for the user, by giving the keyword FREQ a list of n>2 values. This also allows the different sub-bands to be processed in parallel, giving considerable gains in CPU performance (a factor of up to 10 to 20 on the calculation time, and several tens of percent on the memory peak).

For more details, see the generic documents [U2.08.06] on parallelism, and [U2.06.01] on the calculation of mode shapes.
